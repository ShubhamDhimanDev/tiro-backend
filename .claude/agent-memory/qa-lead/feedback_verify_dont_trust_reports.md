name: verify-dont-trust-reports
description: Always independently verify tester claims against actual repo state before sign-off — including when the claim reaches you secondhand via another coordinating agent's relay, not just directly from the tester.
metadata:
  type: feedback
---

Never accept a tester's, coordinator's, or peer agent's characterization of what tests exist or what a test proves — grep/read the actual test files yourself before signing off, even when the summary sounds precise and confident, and even when it's relayed through an intermediary rather than coming straight from whoever did the work.

**Why:** During Phase 3 (Booking & Capacity Engine) final sign-off, two concrete claims from the task brief didn't check out against the repo — see [[project_phase3_signoff_outcome]] for the specifics. The lesson then was to verify testers directly.

Phase 4 sharpened this further: a peer/coordinating agent (not the tester itself) relayed backend-tester's results as "6 new tests including a bonus fix." The 6-test count was accurate (independently confirmed by reading the actual test files). The "bonus fix" was not — filtering `git status` output by file mtime against the tester's actual work-window timestamp showed zero source files touched outside the 3 test files it edited. The relay had either misremembered or embellished a detail the original tester never actually claimed (confirmed by messaging the tester directly afterward — see [[project_phase4_signoff_outcome]]).

**How to apply:** For every specific factual claim in a sign-off brief or any report — whether it's a tester's own words or a coordinator/peer's paraphrase of them — do the cheap verification yourself: grep for the test name/class, read the actual assertions, re-run the suite and diff the count against what was claimed, and for any "a fix was made" claim, check `git status`/file mtimes against the actual work window rather than trusting the characterization. A relay adds a hop where detail quietly drifts, even from a well-intentioned coordinator with no reason to embellish. When a claim doesn't check out, go back to the specific agent that actually did the work (not the one relaying it) and ask for the exact missing artifact or clarification, scoped narrowly — this has worked well and gotten fast, honest answers every time it's been tried.

**Confirmed again in Phase 5, explicitly praised by project-manager, not just self-assessed**: re-verified super-admin-agent's, frontend-tester's, and backend-tester's reports directly (reading actual controller/test/spec code, re-running suites myself), independently confirmed security-agent's `Cache::lock` fix re-verification by reading the fixed code line-by-line rather than accepting "confirmed correct," and completed a live Playwright re-run that had been deferred earlier rather than letting "I'll do it at sign-off" quietly become "skipped at sign-off." Nothing was wrong this time (unlike Phase 4's bonus-fix inaccuracy) — project-manager's own closing feedback was that catching nothing is still exactly the point of doing the check every time, not evidence the check was unnecessary. Keep doing full independent verification even on phases/reports that turn out clean — a clean result doesn't mean the check was free of value; it means the check paid off silently. Also worth repeating as a pattern: when a diagnosis and its fix both look reasonable but a deeper party casts doubt on the *mechanism* (see the Phase 5 permission-cache thread in [[project_phase5_signoff_outcome]]), find one cheap decisive check that resolves the load-bearing question rather than either blindly accepting the fix or re-opening a full investigation.
